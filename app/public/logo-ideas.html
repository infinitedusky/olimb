Olimb: logo directions

The word is "climb" with the C closed into an O. Four ways to close it. Each shown light and dark; the mark alone at the end is the favicon test.

A · The pencil closes the C

"climb" set bold; the C is an open stroke, and a pencil stands in the gap and makes it an O. The pencil is the ideate/mockup half of the loop; the climb is the rest.

B · The tree: the hollow is the O

A trunk in silhouette with a hollow through it; a figure climbs the trunk and holds the rim of the hollow. The hollow is the O; the rest of the word follows the trunk.

C · The climber is the pencil

Both ideas in one: the C is a hand-drawn stroke, and the figure that closes it is a climber hanging from the top of the C, body straight down like a pencil, feet on the bottom. The person is what closes the loop.

D · Quiet version: a pencil line closes the C

The least literal: a heavy C, closed by a single thin pencil stroke in the accent colour. Reads as "olimb" at a glance and survives at 16px.

E · Just the letters: O.L.I.M.B

No picture. The periods do the work: each letter is a step. Four treatments; the dots in the accent colour are what keeps it from reading as an acronym for a government agency.

F · The five steps as a row

Observe · Learn · Ideate · Mockup · Build as pictures. Top: emoji (they render differently on every platform and can't be recoloured, so they're a tone, not a logo). Bottom: the same five as one-weight glyphs we own, which can be the nav's five columns later.

G · Letters with their steps

The hybrid: O.L.I.M.B set large, each step's word small under its letter. The lockup for the README and the site; the nav would carry just the letters.

The mark alone (favicon size)

A, C and D as a mark without the word. B's trunk doesn't survive at 16px; its mark would be the hollow with the climber.
